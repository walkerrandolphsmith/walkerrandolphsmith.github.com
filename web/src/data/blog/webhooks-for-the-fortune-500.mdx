> **“Integration isn’t a feature. It’s infrastructure.”**  
> That change in thinking caused us to redesign how VersionOne communicates with external systems.

VersionOne supports product planning and agile execution for some of the largest enterprises in the world. These include a significant part of the Fortune 500. These teams do more than manage work. They coordinate very large systems of people, tools, and platforms.

More and more, those systems were not isolated. They needed to communicate with each other.

Our customers needed to know, immediately and reliably, when something changed.

So we built **webhook subscriptions**: a flexible, secure, and expressive system. It sends outbound HTTP calls when events occur in VersionOne.

## The Problem: Agile at Enterprise Scale Means Complexity

Our customers use many tools, from CRM to CI/CD and from issue tracking to observability. Something changes in VersionOne: a story moves forward, someone resolves a defect, or a test fails. That change is often the _start_ of a workflow, not the end.

Before this work, integration meant polling our API or building brittle connectors. That did not scale, and it was not real-time.

## What We Built: A Webhooks System with Enterprise DNA

This was more than a wrapper around a pub/sub model. We designed webhook subscriptions for the complexity of enterprise product teams:

- **Fine-Grained Event Modeling**  
  Webhooks can fire on two core event types:

  - `AssetCreated` – when someone creates a new object (such as a Story, Defect, or Test)
  - `AssetChanged` – when any attribute of an object changes

- **Selective Triggering**  
  You can get a notification for _any_ Story status change.  
  You can also get a notification _only_ when the _status_ of a Story in _Project A_, owned by _Susan_, changes.  
  These features make this possible:

  - `attributes` – trigger only on specific fields
  - `filter` + `with` – express detailed conditions
  - `userContext` – respect project-level security boundaries

- **Customizable Payloads**  
  Select the fields that you need (`select`). We send a projection of that asset directly in the `snapshot` of the webhook. Payloads stay clean, relevant, and matched to your needs.

- **Retry Logic & Delivery Guarantees**  
  Each webhook includes a `sequenceId`. External systems use it to put events back in order if they arrive out of order. We also monitor delivery success. After repeated failures, we disable the subscription to stop noisy integrations.

- **Authorization & Metadata**  
  You can configure a webhook with an `authorizationHeader` to meet security requirements. Each payload includes the `instigator`, the person who made the change, with name, email, role, and more.

## How It Works: From Change to Callback

A real use case:

**Trigger**: The Status of a Story in Project X, owned by Susan, changes.  
**Event Definition**:

```json
{
  "type": "AssetChanged",
  "from": "Story",
  "attributes": ["Status"],
  "filter": ["Scope=$Scope"],
  "with": {
    "$Scope": "Scope:0"
  },
  "userContext": "Member:1002",
  "select": ["Name", "Owners.Name"]
}
```

**Result**: The system sends a webhook to the external system of the customer, with this data:

- Who triggered it (Susan)
- What changed (Status from "In Progress" to "Done")
- When (timestamp + sequence)
- Snapshot details (Story name, Owner info)

The customer system can act on that event immediately. It can update dashboards, start workflows, notify teams, and sync with downstream tools.

## What It Enables

This was more than writing JSON to a URL. It enabled modern automation in the ecosystems of our customers:

**Real-time sync** with tools such as Jira, GitHub, Jenkins, and ServiceNow

**Alerting & notifications** in Slack, Teams, or custom UIs

**Automated workflows** across QA, release management, and compliance

Better **traceability**, with system-of-record updates sent to the right people

## Scaling for the Enterprise

We built webhook subscriptions with limits, but we designed them to grow:

- 5 active subscriptions per instance at the start, with a design that allows more
- Simple, secure HTTP + token delivery model
- Fully self-service configuration, so teams can define their own rules
- The same event pipeline infrastructure that we trust internally

Critically, we designed this feature **contract-first**. We knew that customers would build systems on it. So it had to be **predictable**, **observable**, and **future-proof**.

## What We Learned

Expressiveness is essential. Customers do not want a webhook for everything. They want control.

You cannot add security at the end. `userContext` and `authorizationHeader` were mandatory.

Observability matters. Sequence IDs, retry counts, and clear status codes help teams debug fast.

No single integration fits all customers. The more **composable** your system is, the more powerful it becomes for other people.

## Outcomes

**Real-time updates** in mission-critical systems

**Secure, audit-friendly delivery**

**High customer adoption**, especially among enterprise customers with many integrations

**Event reuse** across multiple endpoints, from development workflows to executive reports

Our customers wanted more than updates. They wanted awareness. They wanted VersionOne to take an active part in their ecosystem, not only store work items.

Webhook subscriptions made that possible.

## Conclusion

Software for the Fortune 500 does not operate alone. It is part of an ecosystem of platforms, tools, and people. Each of them needs awareness, context, and control.

We changed events into integrations and changes into callbacks. This gave customers a new way to connect VersionOne to the rest of their tools.

We built more than a feature. We built a foundation.
